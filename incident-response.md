# Incident Response Plan

## Purpose

This document defines a simple response process for security incidents at Mwangaza Research.

The goal is to ensure that security incidents are identified, contained, investigated, resolved, and used as learning opportunities.

---

## Example Incident

A researcher accidentally uploads a spreadsheet containing research participant information to an unapproved public AI tool.

The information may include personal or confidential research information.

This incident may result in unauthorized disclosure of sensitive information.

---

## 1. Detection

The incident may be identified through:

* Employee self-reporting
* Security monitoring
* Manager notification
* Data protection review
* Security awareness activities
* Reports from another employee

### Example

A researcher realizes that they uploaded participant information to an unapproved AI tool and reports the incident to the security team.

---

## 2. Initial Assessment

The security team should determine:

* What information was exposed?
* Whose information was involved?
* Which AI tool was used?
* When did the incident occur?
* Who had access to the information?
* Was the information stored or retained by the AI provider?
* Was the information shared with any other party?
* Are there legal, contractual, privacy, or regulatory requirements that apply?

The incident should then be assigned an appropriate severity level.

### Example Severity

**Severity: High**

Reason:

The incident involves potentially sensitive research participant information being submitted to an unapproved third-party AI service.

---

## 3. Containment

Immediate actions may include:

* Stop further use of the unapproved AI tool
* Prevent additional sensitive information from being uploaded
* Preserve relevant evidence
* Notify the appropriate security or management team
* Review whether the employee account or other systems were affected
* Contact the AI provider where appropriate to understand data handling and deletion options

The goal is to prevent further exposure while preserving evidence.

---

## 4. Investigation

The security team should document:

* What happened
* When it happened
* Who was involved
* What information was affected
* Which systems or services were involved
* How the information was exposed
* Whether the information was accessed or retained
* Existing controls that failed or were bypassed

Relevant evidence may include:

* System logs
* AI tool activity records
* Access records
* Emails or incident reports
* Screenshots
* Data classification information

---

## 5. Remediation

Actions may include:

* Remove or request deletion of exposed information where possible
* Review AI tool approval status
* Review employee access and permissions
* Update AI acceptable-use guidance
* Provide additional security awareness training
* Review data classification procedures
* Add technical controls where appropriate
* Update vendor or AI tool assessment requirements

---

## 6. Recovery

Before normal operations resume, the organization should confirm that:

* The immediate exposure has been contained
* Required notifications have been completed
* Access has been reviewed
* Necessary remediation has been completed
* Relevant evidence has been preserved
* Management has accepted any remaining risk

---

## 7. Lessons Learned

After the incident, the organization should ask:

1. Why did the incident happen?
2. Was the employee aware of the AI restrictions?
3. Was the AI tool clearly classified as approved or restricted?
4. Were data classification rules clear?
5. Could technical controls have prevented the incident?
6. Does the organization need additional AI security training?
7. Should the AI acceptable-use policy be updated?

The lessons learned should be used to reduce the likelihood of similar incidents in the future.

---

## Incident Response Flow

**Detect → Assess → Contain → Investigate → Remediate → Recover → Learn**

This process helps Mwangaza Research respond consistently when security incidents occur.

---

## Key Principle

The objective is not only to respond to an incident.

The objective is to understand why it happened and improve the organization's security controls so that the same type of incident is less likely to happen again.
